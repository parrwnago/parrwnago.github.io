MEMS vibratory gyroscope

The operating principle

A proof mass is driven into oscillation along one axis. An input rotation couples that motion into an orthogonal direction through the Coriolis effect. Differential capacitive sensing converts the small sense-axis displacement into an electrical signal.

The design uses variable-overlap comb drives for actuation and capacitive comb structures for sensing. Matching the drive and sense resonances is central to the calculated response.

Team: Ruturaj Desai, Srujan Kulkarni, Chiang Lee, Parra Wang, and Yanzhao Wang.

Design targets and reported calculations

The course targets were bandwidth greater than 50 Hz and a sensing current greater than 0.1 nA at an input rate of 1°/s. Our report records the following design-stage values.

A process shaped by the geometry

The proposed process starts with a silicon-on-insulator wafer. The structural device layer contains the proof mass, comb fingers, frame, and folded suspension beams.

  1. Define the structural layer

    Use an SOI wafer with a 20 µm device layer and approximately 2 µm buried oxide. Include a conductivity-enhancement step before patterning.

  2. Pattern and etch

    Transfer the planar design through an oxide hard mask, then use deep reactive ion etching to define the silicon structures.

  3. Release the moving structures

    The proposed vapor-HF release removes buried oxide beneath the movable regions while avoiding a liquid-drying step.

  4. Package and tune

    The process plan includes a sealed cavity and possible electrostatic tuning to address drive/sense frequency mismatch after fabrication.

The report identifies a 1.5 µm drive gap through a 20 µm layer, giving an aspect ratio of roughly 13:1. That makes the narrow-gap etch, sidewall geometry, and release process meaningful design constraints rather than details to add after the mechanical model.

What this project adds to my work

This project applies mechanism-design thinking at a different scale. The sensing principle depends on motion, but that motion depends on the spring geometry, material layers, electrode gaps, and the process used to release the structure.

It broadened my experience with electromechanical design and with tracing fabrication constraints back to expected behavior.
