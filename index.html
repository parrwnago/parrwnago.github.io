Current research

Characterizing support-caster behavior in an off-road holonomic robot.

My independent study investigates where ideal robot kinematics stop matching physical behavior—and what the support caster has to do with it.

UPenn · Independent StudyAug. 2025–Present

HAMR / CHUTNI spherical support caster

I integrated a passive split-spherical caster onto the HAMR/CHUTNI platform, refined its mounting, corrected frame and yaw alignment in the ROS 2 / motion-capture pipeline, and organized repeatable logging and analysis for robot motion.

Rather than asking only whether one caster tracks a path better, I am focusing on transient events where mechanism behavior becomes visible: reversal, reorientation, lateral motion, initial caster orientation, slip, and changes in ground contact.

Research question

Which support-caster behaviors create the largest gap between commanded and realized robot motion?

Mechanical designROS 2Vicon / MoCapExperiment designData analysis
A

Mechanism → hypothesis

I use observed hardware behavior to identify specific mechanical effects worth modeling and testing, rather than treating tracking error as a single aggregate metric.

B

Controlled comparison

Current experiments compare spherical and conventional trailing-caster behavior around direction changes, reorientation, rotation, and uneven-terrain contact.

C

Active spherical caster

In parallel, I am designing a standalone actively driven spherical caster. The kinematic derivation is complete and the CAD is in progress toward prototyping and control implementation.

Platform context

Why caster behavior matters

CHUTNI extends a differential-drive / offset-turret architecture to uneven terrain using a rocker suspension and actively stabilized payload. On the physical platform, wheel–terrain interaction, caster alignment, and traction can still limit the motion that the kinematic model predicts.
